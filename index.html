PHYSICIST'S NOTE: THE ANGLE DOUBLING

Why are orthogonal states opposite each other? In textbook Hilbert space, perpendicular states meet at 90°. On the Bloch sphere, angles are doubled (θ/2). Hence, mutually exclusive states |0⟩ and |1⟩ sit 180° antipodal across the diameter!

SHOW THE MATHEMATICS

Spherical coordinates map:

x = r · sin θ · cos φ
y = r · sin θ · sin φ
z = r · cos θ

Density Matrix: ρ = ½ ( I + r · σ ) where σ = (σ_x, σ_y, σ_z) are Pauli spin operators.

The Geometry of One Qubit

Unlike classical bits which are strictly 0 or 1, a quantum bit exists in any linear superposition: |ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩.

Because global phase is physically imperceptible, any single pure qubit can be mapped directly to the surface of a unit sphere (The Bloch Sphere) using two geometric angles:

  • θ (0 to π): Polar angle, the tilt away from |0⟩. Controls the probability balance between |0⟩ and |1⟩.
  • φ (0 to 2π): Longitude. Represents the relative quantum phase angle.

Quantum logic gates are rotations of this sphere. For instance, the Hadamard (H) gate turns the North pole |0⟩ into the balanced equatorial superposition |+⟩.

PHYSICIST'S NOTE: THE ANGLE DOUBLING

In Hilbert space orthogonal states meet at 90°. The sphere doubles every angle: a state at Hilbert angle θ/2 from |0⟩ sits at θ on the sphere. Hence orthogonal states, |0⟩ and |1⟩ among them, sit at opposite ends of a diameter.

SHOW THE MATHEMATICS

Spherical coordinates map:

x = r · sin θ · cos φ
y = r · sin θ · sin φ
z = r · cos θ

Density matrix: ρ = ½ (I + x σx + y σy + z σz), where (x, y, z) is the Bloch vector and r = √(x² + y² + z²) its length.

Outcome probability along a unit axis n: P = (1 + x nx + y ny + z nz) / 2.

The Geometry of One Qubit

Unlike classical bits, which are strictly 0 or 1, a quantum bit can be in any normalised superposition: |ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩, with |α|² + |β|² = 1.

Because normalisation fixes the length and the global phase cannot be observed, any single pure qubit maps to a point on the surface of a unit sphere (the Bloch sphere), given by two angles:

  • θ (0 to π): polar angle, the tilt away from |0⟩. Controls the balance between |0⟩ and |1⟩.
  • φ (0 to 2π): longitude, measured from +X toward +Y. The relative phase between |0⟩ and |1⟩.

Quantum logic gates are rotations of this sphere. For instance, the Hadamard (H) gate turns the North pole |0⟩ into the equatorial superposition |+⟩.

Points inside the sphere are mixed states: uncertainty about which state was prepared, not a superposition. The arrow is a picture of the state, not a direction in the room.
